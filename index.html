Park Hill, Hale and Montclair: where to buy, and what the market is doing

A buyer's guide prepared for you by Jake FreedleBased on MLS sales that closed April 8 to October 1, 2026, plus active and pending listings as of early October 2026

You already know these neighborhoods, so this isn't a tour. It's two things: a look at where value holds up best, and what the last six months of sales say about each price range you might be shopping in.

I drew the lines at 26th Avenue on the north, 6th Avenue on the south, Colorado Boulevard on the west and Quebec Street on the east. That takes in all of Hale and Montclair. On the Park Hill side, your interest in Hale and Park Hill made me think you'd want South Park Hill, so I kept it there rather than reaching farther north.

One honest note on that: South Park Hill technically ends at 23rd Avenue, and the blocks from 23rd to 26th are North Park Hill. I included them on purpose. They run along City Park and the City Park golf course, and living next to City Park is one of the best things about the west side of Park Hill.

There are 60 single-family homes active and 22 pending, which works out to about 2.2 months of inventory. That's a balanced-to-tight market, but the averages hide the most useful pattern in the data: the market is sorting homes into two groups.

41% of homes sold within a week and averaged 101.5% of their original list price. 34% took longer than 30 days and averaged 91.8%. The well-priced, well-located homes still move fast and often above asking. The ones with a problem, whether it's price, condition or location, sit and get discounted. Your job as a buyer is to be ready for the first group and to negotiate hard on the second.

Days on market is MLS days. "Sale vs. original list" compares the final sale price to the first list price, so price reductions count against it. Months of inventory is active listings divided by the average number of sales per month over these six months.

What this means for you

This range is mostly Montclair and Hale: homes from the 1940s and early 1950s (median build year 1944), two or three bedrooms, one or two baths, and about 1,070 square feet above grade. Only 11 of the 54 sales were in Park Hill.

It's also the most split range. The homes that were priced right went fast (41% sold within a week, at an average of 101% of their original price). The rest are sitting. The 23 active listings have a median of 73 days on market, 13 have been up more than 60 days, and 15 have already cut their price. That's where you'll have room to negotiate, and it's worth asking why each one hasn't sold before you jump.

Park Hill is the most competitive of the three right now, with a median of 10 days on market. Montclair has the least inventory. Hale is the softest: 12 of its 22 active single-family listings have been up more than 60 days. If Hale is on your list, that's real leverage, as long as the house isn't sitting for a reason you'd also have to live with.

Townhomes and condos

Attached homes are a small slice of this area, and they're behaving very differently from houses. Over the six months, 9 closed at a median of $700K and 32 days on market. The median sale was 98.3% of original list price, but the average was 94.3% because a few took big reductions.

There are 13 active and 1 pending, which is roughly 8.7 months of inventory, several times what houses have. Actives have a median of 47 days on market and 6 have cut their price. Eleven of the 13 are in Hale, and six of those sit within a couple of blocks of 8th and Colorado, including four built in 2019 asking $1.1M to $1.5M that have been on the market 47 to 199 days.

My read: if a townhome fits how you want to live, you'll have choices and negotiating room. Just go in knowing that resale in this segment is slower than for a house in the same neighborhood, and price your offer with that in mind.

Streets to avoid living on

This is about investment, not just comfort. Even if the noise and traffic don't bother you, they'll bother a big share of the buyers you'll eventually be selling to, and that shows up in your resale price and how long it takes to sell. So my advice is to live at least a few houses off of these streets.

Running east and west

  • 6th Avenue Parkway
  • 8th Avenue
  • Hale Parkway
  • 13th and 14th Avenues
  • Colfax, by at least one full block
  • 17th Avenue Parkway takes some thought. It's a special street, but it still carries a decent amount of through traffic.
  • Montview Boulevard
  • 26th Avenue

Running north and south

  • Colorado Boulevard
  • Holly Street south of 8th
  • Monaco Parkway
  • Quebec Street

For Colorado, Monaco and Quebec, that means not on the street itself, and also not the house one block off whose backyard faces it.

What the last six months show

I flagged every single-family sale whose address is directly on one of these streets, or on a block that touches Colfax. That's 22 of the 160 closed sales.

They're also piling up. Those streets account for 14% of what sold, but 27% of what's for sale right now (16 of 60 active homes). Monaco Parkway alone has 5 active listings against 4 closed sales in six months.

This uses addresses only, so corner lots, homes along Hale Parkway and homes backing to these streets aren't captured. It's a small sample, and size and condition play a part too, but the direction is clear.

Where I'd focus

Some of what makes these pockets valuable may not matter to you day to day: a strong elementary school, a playground, a library branch. That's fine. I'm pointing them out because they make the homes around them more desirable to the widest pool of future buyers, and that's what protects your price when you sell. Close is good. Directly on the busy street next to it is not.

West Park Hill, along City Park

City Park and its golf course run along Colorado from 17th to 26th, with the Denver Zoo and the Museum of Nature & Science inside the park. Being able to walk or bike there is the west side's biggest advantage.

Look a block or more off Colorado, 17th and Montview.

Central South Park Hill, around 23rd and Dexter

Spinelli's Market, Honey Hill Cafe, the Park Hill Community Bookstore and the one-block stretch of Kearney Street between 22nd and 23rd (Cake Crumbs is there) make this Park Hill's best walkable corner. The Park Hill library sits on Montview at Dexter, and Park Hill Elementary is on 19th Avenue.

Homes from roughly 19th to 23rd between Dexter and Grape put all of it within an easy walk. Stay off Montview.

East Park Hill, near Oneida Park

Oneida Street between 22nd and 23rd is a single block of restaurants and shops that works as a gathering spot for the east side of the neighborhood.

Between Monaco and Quebec, a few houses off both.

Hale, within a walk of 9+CO

9+CO at 8th to 9th and Colorado has the AMC, a long list of restaurants, and Trader Joe's is at 8th and Colorado. Lindsley Park, in the middle of Hale, adds a playground, lighted tennis courts and a ball field.

A few blocks east of Colorado gets you the walk without the Colorado traffic. Stay off 8th, 13th, 14th and Hale Parkway.

Montclair, around Montclair Park

Montclair Park at 12th and Oneida and the Montclair School of Academics and Enrichment on Newport anchor the eastern half of the neighborhood.

Between Monaco and Quebec, off 13th and 14th.

Near Colfax and Fairfax, but not too near

Marczyk Fine Foods and Nuggs Ice Cream sit right at Colfax and Fairfax. A specialty grocery and an ice cream shop you can walk to are real selling points for the surrounding blocks on both sides of Colfax.

Keep at least one full block between you and Colfax.

One last thing. The parkways (6th Avenue, 17th Avenue, Montview and Hale) are great walking and running routes, and living a block or two from one is a plus. Living on one is the tradeoff we just talked about.

About the data. All figures come from MLS records for single-family homes, townhomes and condos between Colorado Boulevard and Quebec Street, 6th Avenue and 26th Avenue, priced from $500,000 to $1,500,000. Closed sales run from April 8 to October 1, 2026. Active and pending listings reflect the MLS as of early October 2026.

One closed sale north of 26th Avenue was left out. Eight sales that closed while in coming-soon status were left out of every statistic and are noted by price range. One home listed in two MLS systems was counted once. Homes on Holly Street south of Colfax are counted with Hale. Single-family figures and townhome and condo figures are reported separately.
